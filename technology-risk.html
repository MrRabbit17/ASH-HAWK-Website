Convenience, security, and the cost of speed

01 - Vulnerabilities

Today's Technologies and Their Vulnerabilities

Cybersecurity Reflection

Convenience in exchange for privacy and security

Technology has become so deeply integrated into everyday life that most people rarely stop to consider the risks that accompany it. Despite widespread awareness of cybersecurity threats, modern society continues to prioritise convenience over caution. I believe this contradiction defines the way many of us interact with technology today.

Everyday actions such as unlocking a smartphone with facial recognition, connecting to public Wi-Fi, or downloading free applications involve an implicit exchange: convenience in return for privacy and security. Most users are aware of potential dangers, including phishing attacks, data breaches, identity theft, and software vulnerabilities. However, maintaining constant vigilance is both exhausting and unrealistic in a world that increasingly depends on digital systems.

One reason these risks are so easily overlooked is that modern technology is intentionally designed to feel seamless and invisible. Devices and applications are expected to "just work," while security warnings are often treated as inconveniences that interrupt the user experience. As a result, responsibility for cybersecurity has gradually shifted away from individuals and towards corporations such as Apple, Google, and Microsoft. Although these companies generally provide strong protections, no system is entirely secure, as demonstrated by the frequency of major cyberattacks and data leaks.

Personally, I take certain precautions: I use a password manager, avoid reusing passwords, and regularly update my devices. Nevertheless, I recognise that these measures provide only limited protection against increasingly sophisticated threats.

Ultimately, technological vulnerabilities cannot be eliminated entirely. The more important question is whether society can build systems resilient enough to withstand inevitable failures. Greater public awareness and stronger digital literacy are therefore essential—not to encourage fear, but to promote informed and responsible use of technology.

Strategic Analysis

Progress must be measured by resilience, not speed alone

The rapid evolution of technology has transformed modern society in profound ways, improving communication, healthcare, productivity, and access to information. However, while technological progress offers significant benefits, it also introduces risks that society is struggling to manage effectively. A SWOT analysis provides a useful framework for evaluating both the advantages and the dangers of this constant innovation.

The strengths of technological development are clear. Advancing technologies contribute to economic growth, improve efficiency, and solve complex global problems. Likewise, opportunities such as artificial intelligence in healthcare, renewable energy systems, and global connectivity demonstrate the positive potential of continued innovation. At the same time, there are notable weaknesses, including increasing dependence on technology, planned obsolescence, and the growing concentration of power within a small number of multinational corporations.

In my view, however, the most important aspect of the SWOT analysis is the category of threats. Every new technological layer creates additional vulnerabilities. Each software update, cloud service, or "smart" device expands the number of possible entry points for cyberattacks and system failures. Unlike traditional engineering disciplines, software development still tolerates a culture in which products are frequently released with flaws that are corrected only after deployment. Society would never accept bridges requiring monthly structural repairs, yet regular security patches for essential digital infrastructure have become normalised.

Another major concern is the speed of innovation itself. Technological change often advances more quickly than regulation, ethics, or public understanding can adapt. As a result, societies are frequently forced to respond to consequences only after harm has already occurred.

Technological progress should not be rejected, but it must become more sustainable and responsible. Rather than prioritising speed alone, society should place greater emphasis on resilience, security, and long-term public trust.

Build faster only when you can also build stronger.
