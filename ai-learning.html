Critical Reflection

AI-Supported Learning

Where assistance ends and dependence begins

01 - Tim Protze

Where should we draw the line between using AI to support learning and relying on it to think for us?

Personal Reflection

AI as a guide, not a replacement

Artificial intelligence has become an increasingly common tool in education, offering students new ways to access information, practice skills, and receive personalised support. However, as AI becomes more powerful, an important question arises: where should we draw the line between using AI to support learning and relying on it to think for us?

AI can be a valuable educational resource when it helps students understand complex concepts, generate ideas, or receive feedback on their work. In these situations, it acts as a guide that enhances learning rather than replacing it. For example, a student might use AI to clarify a difficult theory or identify weaknesses in an essay draft. Such uses encourage deeper understanding and independent thinking.

The problem occurs when students depend on AI to complete tasks without engaging with the material themselves. If AI writes essays, solves problems, or makes decisions entirely on a student's behalf, the learning process is undermined. Critical thinking, creativity, and problem-solving skills develop through effort and practice, not through simply accepting answers generated by a machine.

Therefore, the line should be drawn at the point where AI replaces rather than supports human thought. AI should serve as a tool that assists learning, while the responsibility for understanding, analysing, and evaluating information remains with the learner.

02 - Lukas Spitzer

How could teachers redesign assignments so that AI assists but doesn't replace critical thinking?

Pedagogical Strategy

Designing for reasoning, not answers

As artificial intelligence becomes more integrated into education, teachers face the challenge of designing assignments that encourage learning while preventing overreliance on AI. Rather than banning AI completely, educators should create tasks that use it as a tool while still requiring students to demonstrate critical thinking.

One effective approach is to design assignments that emphasise analysis, reflection, and personal interpretation. For example, students could use AI to gather information or generate initial ideas, but they would then be required to evaluate the accuracy of that information and explain their own conclusions. This process encourages students to engage actively with the material instead of simply accepting AI-generated responses.

Teachers can also incorporate real-world experiences, class discussions, and personal observations into assignments. Since AI cannot fully replicate an individual's experiences or perspectives, students must contribute original insights. Additionally, requiring students to document how they used AI during the research and writing process can promote transparency and accountability.

Another useful strategy is to include oral presentations or in-class discussions where students defend their arguments and answer questions. Such activities make it clear whether students genuinely understand the content they have submitted.

Ultimately, assignments should focus on the reasoning behind an answer rather than the answer itself. In this way, AI can support learning while critical thinking remains at the centre of education.

Let AI assist the mind, never replace it.
