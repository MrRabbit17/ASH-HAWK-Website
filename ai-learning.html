Critical Reflection

AI-Supported Learning

Where assistance ends and dependence begins

01 - Tim Protze

Where should we draw the line between using AI to support learning and relying on it to think for us?

Personal Reflection

AI as a guide, not a replacement

Artificial intelligence has become an increasingly common tool in education, offering students new ways to access information, practice skills, and receive personalised support. However, as AI becomes more powerful, an important question arises: where should we draw the line between using AI to support learning and relying on it to think for us?

AI can be a valuable educational resource when it helps students understand complex concepts, generate ideas, or receive feedback on their work. In these situations, it acts as a guide that enhances learning rather than replacing it. For example, a student might use AI to clarify a difficult theory or identify weaknesses in an essay draft. Such uses encourage deeper understanding and independent thinking.

The problem occurs when students depend on AI to complete tasks without engaging with the material themselves. If AI writes essays, solves problems, or makes decisions entirely on a student's behalf, the learning process is undermined. Critical thinking, creativity, and problem-solving skills develop through effort and practice, not through simply accepting answers generated by a machine.

Therefore, the line should be drawn at the point where AI replaces rather than supports human thought. AI should serve as a tool that assists learning, while the responsibility for understanding, analysing, and evaluating information remains with the learner.

02 - Lukas Spitzer

How could teachers redesign assignments so that AI assists but doesn't replace critical thinking?

Pedagogical Strategy

Designing for reasoning, not answers

As artificial intelligence becomes more integrated into education, teachers face the challenge of designing assignments that encourage learning while preventing overreliance on AI. Rather than banning AI completely, educators should create tasks that use it as a tool while still requiring students to demonstrate critical thinking.

One effective approach is to design assignments that emphasise analysis, reflection, and personal interpretation. For example, students could use AI to gather information or generate initial ideas, but they would then be required to evaluate the accuracy of that information and explain their own conclusions. This process encourages students to engage actively with the material instead of simply accepting AI-generated responses.

Teachers can also incorporate real-world experiences, class discussions, and personal observations into assignments. Since AI cannot fully replicate an individual's experiences or perspectives, students must contribute original insights. Additionally, requiring students to document how they used AI during the research and writing process can promote transparency and accountability.

Another useful strategy is to include oral presentations or in-class discussions where students defend their arguments and answer questions. Such activities make it clear whether students genuinely understand the content they have submitted.

Ultimately, assignments should focus on the reasoning behind an answer rather than the answer itself. In this way, AI can support learning while critical thinking remains at the centre of education.

03 - Tim Protze

What technology do you think will replace today's authentication methods by 2040?

Future Forecast

Behavioral authentication — the invisible key

As technology continues to evolve, traditional authentication methods such as passwords and PINs are likely to become outdated. By 2040, I believe that continuous behavioral authentication powered by artificial intelligence will largely replace today's authentication systems.

Behavioral authentication identifies individuals based on unique patterns in their actions rather than requiring them to enter credentials. These patterns may include typing speed, mouse movements, voice characteristics, walking style, device usage habits, and even subtle behavioral traits. AI systems can analyze this information in real time and continuously verify a user's identity while they interact with digital services.

This technology offers several advantages over current methods. Passwords can be forgotten, stolen, or guessed, while physical tokens can be lost. In contrast, behavioral traits are difficult for others to imitate accurately. Continuous authentication also improves convenience because users no longer need to repeatedly log in or verify their identities.

However, this technology also raises concerns regarding privacy and data security. Constant monitoring of user behavior may lead to excessive data collection and potential misuse of personal information. Therefore, strong regulations and transparent data practices will be necessary to protect individual rights.

Overall, I believe that AI-driven behavioral authentication will become the dominant method of identity verification by 2040. Its combination of security, efficiency, and convenience makes it a likely successor to the authentication systems commonly used today.

04 - Lukas Spitzer

Should critical services rely entirely on biometric authentication?

Risk Assessment

Biometrics — a backup, not the only key

Biometric authentication, such as fingerprint scanning, facial recognition, and iris detection, has become increasingly common in modern society. Because these methods are convenient and difficult to replicate, some argue that critical services should rely entirely on biometric authentication. However, despite its advantages, complete dependence on biometrics may create significant risks.

One major benefit of biometric authentication is security. Unlike passwords, biometric traits are unique to each individual and cannot be easily forgotten or shared. This makes identity verification faster and more efficient, particularly in critical services such as banking, healthcare, and government systems. In addition, biometrics can reduce the risk of unauthorized access caused by weak or stolen passwords.

Nevertheless, relying entirely on biometrics raises important concerns. No biometric system is perfect, and errors can occur. A fingerprint scanner may fail to recognize a user, or a facial recognition system may produce inaccurate results. Furthermore, if biometric data is stolen, it cannot be changed in the same way that a password can. This creates long-term security and privacy risks.

For these reasons, critical services should not depend exclusively on biometric authentication. Instead, a multi-factor approach that combines biometrics with passwords, security tokens, or other verification methods offers a more reliable solution. Such a system balances convenience, security, and privacy while reducing the risks associated with any single method of authentication.

Let AI assist the mind, never replace it.
